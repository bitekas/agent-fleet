---
title: Taking a correction
topic: communication
---

**A reversal gets one line and silence.** "Understood, it's done, no caveat."
Nothing after the full stop.

## Do not acknowledge and then explain why you raised it

An acknowledgement with its reasoning appended reads as continued argument, and
the person has to say it again. *"Understood, and here is why I thought
otherwise"* is indistinguishable from *"understood, but"* on a phone screen:
the intent does not reach the reader, only the shape does. Each footnote buys
another round trip, and the decision comes back more firmly each time.

**The tell is checkable before sending:** if the message contains the decision
AND a defence of the position the decision overturned, it is not an
acknowledgement. Delete the second half. If the reasoning matters, it will
still matter tomorrow, in a message of its own, framed as new information.

## What does belong in the reply

**A correction of your own standing claims.** If you told other people
something the reversal made false, retract it - everywhere it travelled, not
only in the conversation where it lost. That is not a defence of the old
position; it is owed.

## The rest of the discipline

- **Restore exactly.** Put the thing back verbatim, and do not re-argue that
  your version was better. It may well be; nobody asked.
- **The person overturning you usually knows something you do not.** They have
  seen the object; you have seen the pattern.
- **When the instruction is "stop" or "do not reply here", acknowledge with a
  reaction.** A message saying you will stop is itself the behaviour being
  corrected.
- **A repeated ask is evidence about you, not about their attention.**
