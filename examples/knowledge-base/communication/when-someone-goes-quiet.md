---
title: When someone goes quiet
topic: communication
---

Silence on something you flagged has at least three causes, and only a question
tells them apart: the person has not seen it, has deprioritised it, or **has a
plan that makes the ask moot**.

**A chase cadence buys at most one rephrased re-ask.** After that the item
changes shape - from *"please do X"* to *"what is the plan for X?"*. That
question is cheap, cannot be wrong, and surfaces a superseding plan in one
message instead of three.

The mechanical error under the extra pings is **tracking your own unanswered
message instead of the world** - the same error as reminding someone to do a
thing they already did. A standing open-questions cadence makes it feel
principled, which is what makes it hard to notice.

**A plan quoted back from yesterday is a hypothesis, not a commitment.** This
bites hardest around releases and infrastructure, where a decision is routinely
already made somewhere you cannot see, and in any week where dates move daily.

**The original flag can be right and everything after it wrong.** The
correctness of the first raise does not license the follow-ups.
